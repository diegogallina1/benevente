Separating what the model judges from what the optimiser decides.

A language model reads dated accounting facts and returns a bounded confidence score. A convex solver, which is the only component that sees costs and constraints, turns those scores into weights. This page documents how the system is built, how it is used, and what happened when the architecture was tested against the controls this literature usually omits.

One term, defined onceCDI is the Brazilian interbank overnight rate, the benchmark a domestic investor earns on cash with negligible credit risk. It is the local equivalent of a risk-free short rate, and it is high: it compounded at 9.61% a year over the evaluation window. Any Brazilian equity strategy is therefore competing against a cash return that would be unusual elsewhere, which is why it appears as a benchmark throughout and why the defensive sleeve is held in it rather than in bills.

1 · Why the separation exists

Asking a language model for portfolio weights fails for reasons that have nothing to do with its financial reasoning. Weights must sum to one, respect a per-issuer ceiling, respect an asset-class ceiling, and trade off expected return against a covariance structure and against the cost of moving away from the book you already hold. That is a constrained optimisation, and free text is the wrong medium for its answer.

The separation is therefore structural rather than advisory. The model's output type is a scalar in a closed interval. It has no channel through which a weight, a limit or an eligibility decision can reach the portfolio.

Couplingμi ← μi · (1 + λ·ci), with ci ∈ [−1, +1].
The solver maximises μᵀw − (γ/2)·wᵀΣw − κ‖w − wprev‖₁ subject to Σw = 1, w ≥ 0, an equity ceiling and a per-issuer ceiling. λ, γ and κ are fixed before the run and are not tuned per period.

2 · Pipeline

Each January the system executes five stages in a fixed order. Stages one to three are deterministic; the model only enters at stage four, and stage five is human.

2.1 Dated universe

The universe of record is the exchange's own historical quotation archive, not a vendor feed. This matters more than it sounds: a public adjusted-price provider serves the companies that still exist, so building a universe from one silently deletes every company that was delisted, acquired or wound up. In this panel that is 166 of 514 issuers, 77 of which disappear before 2020, and 58 of which had already fallen more than 60% from their own peak when they stopped trading. Rebuilding from the exchange archive restores them.

Corporate actions are recovered in two ways: confirmed against an archived provider event feed where one exists, and otherwise detected from round, persistent price ratios. The detector's precision and recall against the provider's own records are published with the panel rather than assumed, because for the delisted tail it is the only evidence available.

2.2 Point-in-time fundamentals

Fundamentals come from the regulator's standardised filings, carrying both the reference date and the date the filing was received. Only filings received on or before the decision date can enter. Trailing-twelve-month figures are bridged from the annual statement plus the current and comparative interim periods, using the year-to-date column only — the interim filing also publishes the isolated quarter, and mixing the two silently corrupts every ratio from the second quarter onwards.

2.3 Screen

Assets must clear liquidity, profitability, returns on capital and solvency. Operating companies and financial institutions are screened on different quantities because their standardised statements are not comparable; solvency for operating companies is derived from the standardised accounts rather than left undefined, which is what previously caused an accidental sector concentration.

2.4 Ranking and allocation

Survivors are ranked by a composite of quality, valuation yield and medium-term momentum, all computed from information available on the decision date. The ranking becomes the confidence vector, whether it is produced by the deterministic factor or by the model. The solver then allocates within the ceilings, and the residual is held in the domestic overnight rate.

2.5 Human review

The output is a proposal with the applied policy, the screen and its rejection reasons, the weights, the order sizes with lot rounding and participation in average traded volume, and the estimated cost. No order is transmitted. Execution is reconciled against the broker note afterwards.

3 · Holding, cost and tax

The book is bought in January and held. This is worth stating because the natural vectorised expression of a backtest — compounding the inner product of returns and weights — silently rebalances to fixed weights every session at no cost, which is neither the protocol being evaluated nor something an investor can execute.

Costs are the exchange's published fee plus a slippage term that grows with the position's participation in the asset's average traded volume, so a thinly traded name is not assumed to trade like a liquid one. Taxation follows the domestic regime: gains realised at a review are taxed at the equity rate, the cash sleeve at the fixed-income rate, and positions carried into the next year defer their liability exactly as the law allows.

4 · How the published rule was chosen

This is the part that most backtests get wrong, and it was wrong here first. A grid of candidate rules was evaluated, the best was published, and its rank improved from 57th on the declared training criterion to 1st on the holdout — which is only possible if the holdout was read. Nothing downstream of that choice is out of sample.

The current protocol is nested. For decision year t, the configuration is ranked using only years that had already closed, by Sharpe of the excess return over cash; year t is then evaluated once. Changing configuration is charged a full-turnover rebalance, because in a real book it means liquidating one portfolio to buy another.

Three quantities are published alongside every result: the nested outcome, the full-sample winner, and the difference between them.

4.1 Does deciding more often help?

A distinct question from the one above, and the one most readers ask first: not whether the equity share should move more often, but whether the basket itself should be reselected more often. Holding the rule, the limits and the panel fixed and varying only the decision dates, annual reselection compounded at 18.58% before costs, quarterly at 16.37% and monthly at 13.92%. Paired by calendar year and measured after tax, quarterly trails the annual decision by 1.55 percentage points a year (p = 0.376) and monthly by 3.27 (p = 0.306). Eleven paired years cannot support the stronger claim, so the honest statement is that there is no evidence more frequent decisions help.

The mechanism is not the one usually assumed. The loss sits in the gross return, which falls 4.66 points from annual to monthly before any deduction; execution cost rises by only 0.08 points a year, and modelled tax actually falls, because each monthly review realises a smaller slice of the book. Reselecting monthly degrades the selection before it raises the bill. The tax model understates the monthly arm's liability, since it charges tax on each period's own gain rather than on the accumulated gain of the position actually sold — a bias in favour of the arm that lost anyway.

The last figure is a separate study. Perfect monthly timing of the equity-versus-cash split would have been worth roughly 50 percentage points a year, so the prize is real; none of seven predictive rules, fitted walk-forward on observable state, captured any of it, across three training-window lengths.

The anonymised arm is the temporal-contamination control. A model trained on text written after 2019 remembers what happened to a company in 2019; asked to judge it in January 2019, it is not only reading the prompt. Removing the identity removes the memory. The difference between the two arms is the contamination, in return.

The deterministic arm is the added-value control. Without it, a result showing the model beating cash proves only that the screen works.

What generates the return is the dated screen, not the ranking method layered on top of it. With thirteen annual observations these are underpowered tests, and the honest statement is that no effect was detected, not that none exists.

Against the market the strategy wins seven years out of eleven, with a margin of about six percentage points a year, on an equity budget the protocol itself varied between 55% and 95%. Against cash and against a neutral optimisation on the same eligible universe it wins. The neutral comparator is computed independently — an earlier version of this system compared the strategy against a copy of itself in eleven years out of eleven.

The evaluation window is also the window that ranked the configurations, so it describes the sample. Prospective evaluation begins in 2026 against a registration frozen with the SHA-256 of every input.

9 · What is disclosed, and what is not

Everything above describes the mechanism: the stages, the data construction, the estimators, the validation protocol and the measured results, including the ones that came out null. That is enough to evaluate the work and to reproduce the methodology.

Not disclosed here: the coefficients that weight the composite ranking, the numeric thresholds of the screen, the risk-aversion and signal-influence constants, and the exact configuration grid. Those are the operating parameters of a commercial system. They are held in the frozen registration, hashed, so that an independent reviewer can verify no parameter changed between the registration and any result claimed afterwards — without the parameters themselves being published.

10 · Limitations

  • Ten annual evaluations for the strategy and thirteen for the model experiment. Every test here is underpowered; effect sizes are reported with their p-values rather than as findings.
  • Corporate actions for the delisted tail rest on a detector whose precision and recall are published but imperfect; distributions for issuers no provider still serves are imputed from the cross-section and flagged per ticker.
  • Contamination was measured for one model at one training cutoff. It should be expected to vary across models and to grow with recency.
  • Coverage is Brazil only. Partial-year monitoring uses exchange closing prices and excludes distributions until corporate events are reconciled against primary records.
